# EA Almanac — Expert Research Method

## Purpose

The Almanac is being built as a developmental reference library, not as a collection of generic parenting tips. Each age band is treated as a small research project.

For each age band and each category, the process is:

1. **Define the developmental question.** What is actually changing during this season?
2. **Establish the evidence base.** Use developmental science, pediatric guidance, early-childhood education research, and authoritative public-health material where applicable.
3. **Separate evidence from philosophy.** Evidence-backed guidance, professional consensus, educational philosophy, cultural practice, and practical parent wisdom must not be blended as though they have equal evidentiary status.
4. **Translate knowledge into atomic entries.** Each entry should answer one useful question or provide one actionable idea.
5. **Cross-check against adjacent ages.** A 1–2 entry should feel different from infancy and different from 2–5; a 2–5 entry should not quietly assume kindergarten-level abilities.
6. **Build for retrieval.** Entries need tags and concise wording so the same knowledge can serve daily, weekly, monthly, and thematic queries.
7. **Review for parent usefulness.** The final question is: would this help a parent notice something important, respond better, or create a worthwhile opportunity?

## Source hierarchy

### Tier A — Primary / authoritative

- CDC developmental milestones and public-health guidance
- Government health and child-development guidance
- Professional pediatric organizations
- Major professional early-childhood organizations
- Peer-reviewed systematic reviews and high-quality research

### Tier B — Expert synthesis

- Harvard Center on the Developing Child
- Established university child-development centers
- Major evidence-informed parenting/development organizations
- Professional textbooks and reference works

### Tier C — Practical / cultural / philosophical

- Recognized parenting and education philosophies
- Practical family wisdom
- High-quality activity and book recommendations
- Cultural practices and traditions

Tier C material may be valuable, but it must not be presented as settled developmental science.

## Category-specific expert lenses

| Category | Primary question |
|---|---|
| Experiencing | What is the child likely experiencing internally, cognitively, socially, and physically? |
| Needs | What conditions, relationships, environments, and supports make healthy development easier? |
| Teach | What useful knowledge or capability can a child reasonably begin practicing now? |
| Encourage | Which emerging interests, behaviors, dispositions, and habits are worth reinforcing? |
| Avoid | Which adult responses or environmental patterns commonly work against development? |
| Healthy Development | What observable patterns are reassuring without becoming a checklist or diagnosis? |
| Warning Signs | What persistent or significant patterns justify closer attention or professional discussion? |
| Weekly Focus | What small theme can a family practice for roughly one week? |
| Monthly Focus | What broader developmental theme can organize several weeks of family life? |
| Books | Which child and parent books provide useful developmentally appropriate experiences or knowledge? |
| Activities | What can the child actually do that creates practice, discovery, connection, or joy? |
| Conversations | What language-rich interactions help the parent understand and support the child? |
| Life Skills | What practical capabilities can gradually move from adult-supported to child-participated? |
| Opportunities | What experiences, communities, outings, programs, hobbies, or responsibilities could widen the child's world? |
| Parent Reminders | What does the parent need to remember about the child's developmental reality or their own response? |

## Safety rule

Warning signs are never diagnoses. A single missed skill or unusual behavior should not automatically be treated as pathology. Entries should emphasize persistence, loss of previously acquired skills, clusters of concerns, functional impact, or professional guidance where appropriate.

## Current research anchors

- CDC milestone guidance establishes age-specific developmental reference points and explicitly notes that its milestone lists are not substitutes for standardized developmental screening. The CDC places milestones at ages by which at least 75% of children are expected to demonstrate them.
- Harvard Center on the Developing Child provides evidence-informed material on responsive relationships, serve-and-return interaction, brain architecture, executive function, stress, and play.
- NAEYC's Developmentally Appropriate Practice framework emphasizes strengths-based, play-based learning while accounting for commonality, individuality, culture, language, ability, and context.

## Prototype production rule

The first repository population is a **research-backed prototype layer**, not the final 1,175-entry ceiling. Initial entries should prove the schema and voice. Expansion happens category-by-category after the retrieval model is tested.
