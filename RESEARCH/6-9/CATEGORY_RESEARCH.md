# 6–9 Category Research Pass

The categories are researched independently because they answer different questions. Shared developmental evidence may recur, but the editorial question changes.

| Category | Independent research question | Primary evidence emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Experiencing | What is the child increasingly noticing, attempting, comparing, feeling, and organizing? | Developmental science, middle-childhood psychology |
| Needs | What conditions help this child learn and develop without over- or under-supporting them? | Developmental science, education, health |
| Teach | Which capabilities can be intentionally taught and practiced? | Education evidence, SEL, practical skill frameworks |
| Encourage | Which interests, behaviours, and approaches are worth making room for? | Motivation, play, learning, physical activity |
| Avoid | Which adult responses or environmental patterns undermine development? | Professional guidance; conservative framing |
| Healthy Development | What observable patterns can reassure a parent that development is broadly progressing? | Professional developmental guidance |
| Warning Signs | What persistent patterns warrant discussion with school/health professionals? | Pediatric/developmental guidance only; non-diagnostic |
| Weekly Focus | What seven-day theme can translate developmental knowledge into action? | Synthesis of researched categories |
| Monthly Focus | What broader developmental theme deserves sustained attention? | Synthesis; developmental season |
| Books | What reading material offers age-fit pleasure, knowledge, language, or perspective? | Library/educational sources; content fit |
| Activities | What concrete experiences let children practice skills while retaining agency? | Play, education, physical activity |
| Conversations | What prompts can deepen thinking, language, perspective, and connection? | Language, SEL, family communication |
| Life Skills | Which everyday responsibilities can become progressively independent? | Practical-life and family guidance |
| Opportunities | What real-world settings provide authentic practice and belonging? | Community, sport, arts, service, clubs |
| Parent Reminders | What should the adult remember about this developmental season? | Cross-category synthesis and professional guidance |

## Research conclusions informing the prototype

1. **Competence must not become worth.** The corpus should distinguish "I can improve at this" from "I am valuable because I am good at this."
2. **Development remains multidomain.** NAEYC emphasizes that cognitive, social, emotional, physical, and linguistic development are interrelated; the Almanac should not become a school-only database. [`NAEYC-DAP`]
3. **Play remains useful.** NAEYC describes play and guided play as vehicles for self-regulation, language, problem-solving, peer interaction, and learning, including in primary grades. [`NAEYC-DAP`, `NAEYC-PRIMARY-PLAY`]
4. **SEL is teachable.** CASEL organizes social-emotional development around competencies such as self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, and responsible decision-making. [`CASEL-FRAMEWORKS`]
5. **Academic support should be concrete.** IES guidance supports systematic elementary mathematics instruction, mathematical language, and well-chosen representations rather than relying on vague encouragement. [`IES-MATH-ELEM`]
6. **Health is part of competence.** Sleep and movement should be treated as foundations for learning and wellbeing, not as rewards after schoolwork. AASM recommends 9–12 hours of sleep for ages 6–12; WHO recommends an average of at least 60 minutes/day of moderate-to-vigorous activity for ages 5–17. [`AASM-SLEEP`, `WHO-PA-5-17`]
7. **Digital life belongs in the model.** AAP recommends family media planning, screen-free contexts, attention to content, and revisiting rules as children mature. [`AAP-MEDIA-SCHOOLAGE`, `AAP-MEDIA-PLAN-2026`]
8. **Food guidance should avoid moralizing.** AAP frames the parent's role as providing healthy options and the child's role in deciding how much to eat; the corpus should avoid calorie policing or body-shame language. [`AAP-NUTRITION-GRADESCHOOL`]

## Prototype boundary

The first 150-entry tranche covers `experiencing`, `needs`, `teach`, `encourage`, and `avoid`. `healthy_development` and `warning_signs` are deliberately held for a separate higher-scrutiny pass.
