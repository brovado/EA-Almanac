# 6–9 Prototype QA — Round 1

## Corpus tranche

- Experiencing: 25
- Needs: 25
- Teach: 50
- Encourage: 25
- Avoid: 25
- Total: 150

## Structural checks

### PASS — Stable IDs
All prototype entries use category-specific stable IDs (`E6`, `N6`, `T6`, `C6`, `A6`).

### PASS — Category separation
Each entry has exactly one category and was written for that category's independent question. Shared themes are allowed; duplicate utility is not.

### PASS — Primary domain
Each entry has one primary domain from the 6–9 domain model.

### PASS — Window model
Entries use `6-6.9`, `7-7.9`, `8-9.9`, or `6-9-broad`. Broad entries are used where the idea is not meaningfully tied to a narrower age.

### PASS — Atomicity
Entries generally express one observation, condition, capability, encouragement, or adult response. Composite entries are candidates for semantic QA.

### PASS — Source type
Each entry identifies whether its framing is evidence, professional guidance, educational framework, or practical wisdom.

### HOLD — Source IDs
The prototype uses source types but not individual `source_ids` on every row. Before the final corpus, evidence-sensitive entries should be mapped to the source registry at entry level.

### HOLD — Domain balance
The prototype is intentionally research-shaped rather than quota-shaped. Final corpus expansion should audit domain distribution so academic material does not crowd out social, emotional, physical, responsibility, creativity, and practical-life material.

### HOLD — Window precision
Some developmental observations currently use broad windows. During window QA, only narrow entries that have a defensible age rationale should be narrowed.

### PASS — Safety boundary
`healthy_development` and `warning_signs` were excluded from this first tranche. Those categories will receive separate higher-scrutiny research before being populated.

## Refinement loop

This is not the final 6–9 corpus. The next passes should be:

1. Structural QA — complete for prototype; repeat after expansion.
2. Semantic QA — identify functional near-duplicates and generic advice.
3. Developmental QA — challenge age-window assignments and developmental claims.
4. Source QA — attach source IDs to evidence-sensitive claims.
5. Snapshot QA — generate daily/weekly examples and inspect usefulness.
6. Expansion — only after the prototype survives those tests.

## Research caution

CDC states that its milestone checklists are communication tools and not developmental standards. The Almanac therefore must not turn broad developmental patterns into deadlines or diagnostic judgments. `CDC-2026-MILESTONES` is used as a methodological caution rather than as a 6–9 milestone source.
