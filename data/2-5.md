# 2–5 — Exploration & Agency

**Developmental season:** rapidly expanding language, symbolic thought, pretend play, emerging self-control, social learning, competence, identity, and increasingly deliberate participation.

**Evidence anchors:** CDC 2-, 3-, 4-, and 5-year milestones; Harvard serve-and-return, play, executive-function and stress resources; NAEYC developmentally appropriate practice.

> Prototype layer: 5 atomic entries per category. This is intentionally the first research tranche; categories will be expanded toward their full density targets after retrieval testing.

## 1. Experiencing

| ID | Entry |
|---|---|
| E25-001 | The child increasingly experiences themselves as an agent who can initiate actions, make choices, and influence what happens. |
| E25-002 | Pretend play becomes a major way to rehearse social roles, language, problem-solving, and emotional experiences. |
| E25-003 | Feelings can be intense while the ability to inhibit an impulse is still developing. |
| E25-004 | The child is becoming better at representing absent things with words, drawings, objects, and stories. |
| E25-005 | Social comparison and awareness of other people's reactions become increasingly meaningful during the preschool years. |

## 2. Needs

| ID | Entry |
|---|---|
| N25-001 | Warm, responsive relationships that combine emotional security with room to explore. |
| N25-002 | Opportunities for sustained play, especially pretend, constructive, physical, and social play. |
| N25-003 | Adults who provide predictable boundaries while allowing genuine choices inside those boundaries. |
| N25-004 | Rich language interaction: conversation, storytelling, reading, questions, and explanations. |
| N25-005 | Repeated practice with emotional regulation, conflict repair, waiting, and flexible problem-solving. |

## 3. What To Teach

| ID | Entry |
|---|---|
| T25-001 | Teach the names of common emotions and simple ways to describe intensity. |
| T25-002 | Teach that rules can exist to protect people, make activities work, or help groups cooperate. |
| T25-003 | Teach simple household responsibilities as real contributions rather than pretend chores. |
| T25-004 | Teach how to ask for help, disagree respectfully, and try another strategy when the first fails. |
| T25-005 | Teach basic counting through meaningful objects and everyday activities rather than drill alone. |
| T25-006 | Teach the structure of simple stories: beginning, what happened, and what happened next. |
| T25-007 | Teach body-safety language and the idea that children can tell a trusted adult when something feels wrong. |
| T25-008 | Teach simple conflict scripts such as "stop," "I don't like that," "can I have a turn?" and "let's trade." |
| T25-009 | Teach basic care of belongings: where things live, how to carry them, and how to put them away. |
| T25-010 | Teach early time language through routines: before, after, today, tomorrow, morning, night. |

## 4. What To Encourage

| ID | Entry |
|---|---|
| C25-001 | Encourage questions even when the parent does not immediately know the answer. |
| C25-002 | Encourage pretend play without over-directing the story. |
| C25-003 | Encourage persistence and strategy changes rather than praising only successful outcomes. |
| C25-004 | Encourage children to contribute to family routines. |
| C25-005 | Encourage storytelling, drawing, building, music, movement, and making things. |
| C25-006 | Encourage noticing other people's feelings without requiring the child to become responsible for them. |
| C25-007 | Encourage outdoor exploration and physically active play. |
| C25-008 | Encourage early independence in dressing, hygiene, food preparation, and tidying at an appropriate level. |
| C25-009 | Encourage curiosity about nature, machines, animals, numbers, letters, and how things work. |
| C25-010 | Encourage repair after conflict: reconnect, name what happened, and try again. |

## 5. What To Avoid

| ID | Entry |
|---|---|
| A25-001 | Avoid treating normal preschool impulsivity as deliberate moral failure. |
| A25-002 | Avoid replacing play with excessive academic drilling when the child is still learning effectively through active experiences. |
| A25-003 | Avoid humiliating children for accidents, fears, mistakes, or immature emotional control. |
| A25-004 | Avoid asking for apologies as a performance without helping the child understand repair. |
| A25-005 | Avoid solving every small problem before the child has had a chance to think. |

## 6. Signs Of Healthy Development

| ID | Entry |
|---|---|

| H25-003 | Increasing ability to follow simple rules and routines with adult support. |

## 7. Warning Signs

| ID | Entry |
|---|---|
| W25-001 | Loss of previously acquired skills should be discussed promptly with a healthcare professional. |
| W25-002 | Persistent concerns across language, social interaction, movement, or learning deserve developmental discussion rather than assumptions about motivation. |
| W25-003 | Persistent difficulty being understood, communicating needs, or engaging socially should be raised with a professional when it meaningfully interferes with daily life. |
| W25-004 | Persistent inability to follow age-expected routines or participate in ordinary activities, especially across settings, may warrant closer assessment. |
| W25-005 | Repeated parental concern is itself useful information and is worth bringing to a child's healthcare provider or educator. |

## 15. Parent Reminders

| ID | Reminder |
|---|---|
| PR25-001 | The goal is not to eliminate every frustration; it is to help the child become increasingly capable of moving through frustration. |
| PR25-002 | A child can understand a rule and still fail to follow it when excited, tired, or overwhelmed. |
| PR25-003 | Questions are often more educational than answers because they invite the child into the thinking process. |
| PR25-004 | Preschool competence grows through repeated real participation, not through lectures about responsibility. |
| PR25-005 | Play is not the absence of learning; play is one of the child's major learning environments. |
| PR25-006 | Let the child struggle a little when the struggle is safe and productive. |
| PR25-007 | Do not confuse obedience with self-regulation; a child who complies may still need help understanding and managing feelings. |
| PR25-008 | Your job is gradually changing from doing things for the child toward doing things with the child. |
| PR25-009 | Children borrow our language for emotions, conflict, curiosity, and problem-solving before they can reliably generate it themselves. |
| PR25-010 | Preserve wonder. Not every question needs to become a lesson. |
