# 1–2 — Emerging Independence

**Developmental season:** rapid mobility, imitation, emerging language, attachment + exploration, early autonomy, immature self-regulation.

**Evidence anchors:** CDC 12–24 month milestones; CDC positive-parenting guidance; Harvard serve-and-return; NAEYC developmentally appropriate practice.

> Prototype layer: 5 atomic entries per category. This is intentionally the first research tranche; categories will be expanded toward their full density targets after retrieval testing.

## 1. Experiencing

| ID | Entry |
|---|---|
| E12-001 | Movement becomes a major way of learning; walking, carrying, climbing, pushing, and reaching are not merely physical skills but ways to investigate the world. |
| E12-002 | The child increasingly notices that adults and older children can be copied, making imitation a powerful route into learning. |
| E12-003 | Understanding is often ahead of expressive language; a child may know what they want long before they can explain it. |
| E12-004 | The child is beginning to experience themselves as a separate person with preferences and influence. |
| E12-005 | Big emotions can arrive faster than the child's ability to regulate them; needing help calming is developmentally ordinary. |

## 2. Needs

| ID | Entry |
|---|---|
| N12-001 | A secure adult base from which to explore and return for reassurance. |
| N12-002 | Frequent back-and-forth interaction: sounds, gestures, pointing, naming, copying, and simple conversation. |
| N12-003 | Safe physical freedom to move, manipulate objects, and investigate cause and effect. |
| N12-004 | Predictable routines with enough flexibility for exploration. |
| N12-005 | Adults who provide simple limits while remaining emotionally available during frustration. |

## 3. What To Teach

| ID | Entry |
|---|---|
| T12-001 | Teach simple body-part names during dressing, bathing, songs, and play. |
| T12-002 | Teach a small functional vocabulary for needs: more, help, up, down, finished, open, water. |
| T12-003 | Teach simple safety boundaries through repetition: stop, hot, sharp, street, gentle. |
| T12-004 | Teach that objects have names, locations, and simple functions by narrating everyday routines. |
| T12-005 | Teach simple turn-taking through very short games with a predictable back-and-forth pattern. |
| T12-006 | Teach how to participate in routines such as putting a toy away or bringing an item to an adult. |
| T12-007 | Teach gentle handling of pets, people, and fragile objects through immediate modelling. |
| T12-008 | Teach basic choice-making by offering two acceptable options. |
| T12-009 | Teach simple gestures and words for requesting help instead of expecting perfect verbal communication. |
| T12-010 | Teach that books can be handled, looked at, pointed to, and talked about rather than requiring passive listening. |

## 4. What To Encourage

| ID | Entry |
|---|---|
| C12-001 | Encourage safe exploration rather than interrupting every unfamiliar action. |
| C12-002 | Encourage attempts at self-feeding, even when the result is messy. |
| C12-003 | Encourage helping with tiny household tasks. |
| C12-004 | Encourage pointing, showing, bringing, and sharing attention with an adult. |
| C12-005 | Encourage persistence by giving the child a moment to try before stepping in. |
| C12-006 | Encourage imitation of useful adult actions. |
| C12-007 | Encourage movement through safe pushing, carrying, climbing, and ball play. |
| C12-008 | Encourage interest in music, rhythm, and simple action songs. |
| C12-009 | Encourage affection and reconnection after ordinary separations. |
| C12-010 | Encourage curiosity with everyday objects instead of relying only on purpose-built toys. |

## 5. What To Avoid

| ID | Entry |
|---|---|
| A12-001 | Avoid expecting impulse control that the child's developing self-regulation cannot reliably provide. |
| A12-002 | Avoid turning every harmless exploration into a prohibition. |
| A12-003 | Avoid shaming normal mess, clumsiness, or early failed attempts at independence. |
| A12-004 | Avoid using long explanations when a short boundary plus redirection will work better. |
| A12-005 | Avoid treating limited speech as evidence that the child has limited understanding. |

## 6. Signs Of Healthy Development

| ID | Entry |
|---|---|
| H12-001 | Uses movement and exploration to investigate new environments. |
| H12-002 | Seeks an adult for reassurance and can return to exploration after support. |
| H12-003 | Increasingly uses gestures, sounds, words, or shared attention to communicate. |
| H12-004 | Imitates familiar actions during play and routines. |
| H12-005 | Gradually adds participation in simple everyday tasks. |

## 7. Warning Signs

| ID | Entry |
|---|---|
| W12-001 | Loss of a previously acquired developmental skill warrants prompt discussion with a healthcare professional. |
| W12-002 | Persistent lack of response to sounds, voices, or social interaction deserves discussion rather than simple wait-and-see. |
| W12-003 | Persistent concerns across communication, movement, or social interaction are worth raising during developmental screening. |
| W12-004 | Significant difficulty using gestures or other ways to communicate needs should be discussed with a professional if persistent. |
| W12-005 | A parent who repeatedly feels that development is not progressing should bring that concern to the child's healthcare provider even if others are reassuring. |

| PR12-004 | Repetition is not wasted time; repetition is how young children consolidate learning. |

| PR12-010 | Independence begins as participation long before it looks like doing a task alone. |
